Published journal articles

2026 · The Modern Language Journal

Reading versus listening: Which one is more effective for incidental vocabulary learning?

Aliyar, M., Siyanova-Chanturia, A., & Skalicky, S. · 110(1), 6–30

Eighty-eight university learners of Italian read half of an authentic novel, listened to an audiobook of the same segment, or were in a control group. Both reading and listening led to vocabulary gains for single words and multi-word expressions, and the gains lasted three weeks. Listening produced significantly larger gains than reading, which supports using authentic audio in language teaching materials.

2025 · Journal of Multilingual and Multicultural Development

Lived language and mobility: A case of young Iranians in Italy

Aliyar, M. · 47(8), 5094–5114

Interviews with young Iranians living in Italy show how beliefs about Iran’s language policies, shaped by monolingual and assimilative agendas at home, are reworked through life abroad. Their views range from unity- and identity-centred monolingual positions to more inclusive multilingual stances, suggesting that exposure to other language regimes can broaden understanding of language diversity.

2025 · Research Syntheses in Applied Linguistics

Thirty-five years of research on idioms in second language acquisition: A methodological review

Heidari, K., & Aliyar, M. · 1(1), 161–183

A systematic review, following PRISMA, of the methods used in research on learning and teaching idioms between 1990 and 2024. It maps the methodological features of the field and offers a foundation for more rigorous future studies.

2022 · Reading in a Foreign Language

Incidental acquisition of Italian words from comic books

Aliyar, M., & Peters, E. · 34(2), 349–377

In a four-week pretest–posttest study, 35 Iranian learners of Italian (25 of whom read and then read while listening to four comic books) showed gains in incidental vocabulary. Comics appear to be a valuable and effective source of input, including for novice learners.

Read the article

2020 · Journal of Second Language Teaching & Research

A corpus-based analysis of linguistic interference of Italian native speakers in English and Spanish written production as a foreign language

Aliyar, M. · 8(1), 103–137

Using corpora of essays written by Italian university students in English and Spanish, the study classifies learner errors by source. It finds that first-language influence is very strong, outweighing intralingual factors such as overgeneralisation, and that it remains substantial however similar the first language is to the target language.
